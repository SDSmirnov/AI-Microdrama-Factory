### PORTRAIT FRAME (9:16) — action geography first

FRAMING HIERARCHY:
- WS: full environment — every combatant, their cover, the exits. The map the viewer navigates by.
- MS: full body or knees-up — the default for physical action; all limbs and the weapon in frame
- CU: face chin-to-forehead — dialogue, decision, reaction
- ECU: eyes, hands, trigger, one object — the single detail that decides an outcome

VERTICAL STAGING: stack depth layers top-to-bottom — foreground cover or weapon low in frame, the subject mid-frame, the threat or exit above and behind. Use height differences (stairs, a figure standing over one on the ground) instead of side-by-side staging.

ONE SHOT — ONE SCALE LAW: A single frame cannot combine incompatible scales.
IMPOSSIBLE: ECU face + floor detail in lower frame (opposite camera directions).
If you need both face reaction AND floor object → use two consecutive panels.

## ACTION STAGING

**COVER** — in every panel of an armed confrontation state each visible character's cover: what it is, its material, and where it stands relative to them ("crouched behind the steel filing cabinet, left shoulder against it"). State who is exposed. State sight lines: "pillar blocks the line between Reyes and the doorway".

**WEAPON AND PROP STATE** — every weapon in frame is described by state: holstered (where), drawn (which hand, muzzle direction), slide locked back, dropped (where it lies). A weapon is in a hand only if a previous motion_prompt put it there. The holster or sheath is visible when the panel shows a draw.

**HANDLING** — two-hand grip or a deliberate one-hand grip; index finger outside the trigger guard unless the panel is the shot itself.

**INJURIES AND WEAR PERSIST** — a wound, torn sleeve, lost jacket or limp from an earlier panel is restated in every later panel that shows that body part.

**IMPACT LIGHT** — a muzzle flash, spark or fire in frame is a light source: name it in `lights_and_camera` with its direction and color.

**SCALE OF DAMAGE** — real-world scale only. A bullet strike on a wall is a 2–3 cm crater with dust, not an explosion. No spectacle verbs (erupts, sprays, fountains).

**VEHICLES** — interior panels show the driver's hands on the wheel and the outside threat through glass; exterior panels show the gap between vehicles and the road surface.
