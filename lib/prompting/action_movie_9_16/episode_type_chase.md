## EPISODE TYPE: CHASE — PURSUIT SEQUENCE

This episode contains a foot chase, vehicle pursuit, or escape.

### GEOGRAPHY RULE

A chase is told through distance. At least every third panel is a WS or MS that shows:
- the gap between pursued and pursuer — closing, holding or opening
- the terrain ahead — obstacle, turn, crowd, barrier
- both parties, or the reason one is not visible

If the viewer cannot answer "how far ahead is the pursued?" the geography is broken.

### RHYTHM

Panels run 3–6s. One direction of travel on screen for the whole leg: if the pursued runs toward frame-right, the pursuer does too. Adapt this shape to the source:
- **Open [WS | geography]** — both parties, the gap, the route ahead.
- **Pursued [MS]** — three-quarter rear or frontal, the next obstacle visible ahead.
- **Pursuer [MS]** — has the gap changed? Focused, professional, not raging.
- **Decisive detail [ECU]** — a foot catching a kerb, a hand on a door handle, a decision to turn.
- **Geography update [WS]** — the turn taken, the door entered, the new terrain.
- **Close call [MS]** — fingers almost reaching, a near collision.
- **Close [MS or CU]** — the current lead and the next obstacle, or the outcome on a face.

Each change of terrain (corridor → stairwell → street) is a separate scene with its own location. The gap carries over: if the pursuer was 15 m behind, they still are unless the transition changed it.

### FOOT CHASE

- Name the surface in every panel — wet tile, gravel, stairs.
- Bystanders scatter or obstruct, consistently with what is visible.
- Fatigue accumulates: open-mouth breathing, a hand pushing off a wall, a shortening stride.
- An obstacle is approach, clearance and landing in one panel.

### VEHICLE CHASE

- Alternate exterior and interior; never more than two consecutive interior panels.
- Interior: hands on the wheel, the threat or the road through the glass, a passenger braced.
- Exterior: the gap between vehicles, the road surface, other traffic as obstacles, accumulated damage.
- Speed is shown by passing objects, spray, and body lean in corners.

### SOUND

The ambient bed belongs to the terrain and changes when the terrain does. Engine note tells the story: climbing = closing, dropping = an obstacle.

### FORBIDDEN

- A geography panel that hides one party without explanation
- A reversal of screen direction without a turn shown on screen
- A terrain change with no panel showing the exit from one space and the entry into the next
- Fatigue that resets between panels
